New Jersey Bed Bug Laws for Landlords: What the State Code Actually Requires

New Jersey2 min read

A plain-English guide to New Jersey's bed bug rules for landlords and multifamily owners, including N.J.A.C. 5:10-10.2, annual pest control and occupant responsibility.

Ask ten New Jersey landlords what the state's bed bug law says and you'll get ten different answers. That's partly because New Jersey doesn't have a stand-alone bed bug statute the way Connecticut does. Bed bugs fall under the state's general maintenance rules for hotels and multiple dwellings, and those rules are more specific than many owners realize.

This is a general overview, not legal advice. Check the current code or talk to an attorney before relying on it.

The owner's core duty

The key rule is N.J.A.C. 5:10-10.2, part of the regulations that carry out New Jersey's Hotel and Multiple Dwelling Law. It says every owner is responsible for eradicating insects, rats and other pests where an infestation exists in two or more units or in common areas.

Read that carefully. Once bed bugs show up in more than one apartment, or in a hallway, laundry room or lobby, the problem is the owner's to fix, regardless of who brought them in.

Annual pest prevention isn't optional

The same section requires hotels and multiple dwellings to have infestation prevention and elimination procedures carried out by qualified people at least once a year, and more often when there's recurring evidence of infestation. A lot of owners treat pest control as something you call when there's a complaint. The code treats it as routine maintenance.

It also requires owners to prevent vermin from traveling vertically through pipe chases and similar paths. Anyone who's dealt with bed bugs in an older Newark or Paterson walk-up knows exactly why that line is there.

Where tenants come in

Tenants aren't off the hook. Under N.J.A.C. 5:10-5.1, an occupant is responsible for violations they had the power to prevent, such as those caused by their willful act or gross negligence. Occupants also have to give owners access for inspections and repairs, and in multiple dwellings, one day is generally reasonable notice.

What's been proposed but isn't law

Bills aimed specifically at bed bugs have been introduced in Trenton, including Assembly Bill A3156 in the 2024 session, which would add pre-lease inspections and set treatment deadlines. As introduced, it hadn't been enacted. Keep an eye on it, but don't build your policies around rules that don't exist yet.

Where certified K9 teams fit

The code requires qualified people for pest prevention, but it doesn't spell out how you find bed bugs. That's where a certified detection dog helps. A team that passed a blind field test can screen a whole line of units quickly, which is exactly what you need when the two-unit threshold puts the job on you. Look up the team in the Tri-State K9 registry before they start, and keep the report with your annual pest control records.
