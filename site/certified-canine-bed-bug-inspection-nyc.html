What to Expect From a Certified Canine Bed Bug Inspection in NYC

For property managers2 min read

A walk-through of a certified K9 bed bug inspection in a New York City building, from tenant notice and prep to the search and the report.

If you've never had a K9 bed bug inspection in one of your buildings, it can be hard to know what you're actually paying for. Here's how a typical job goes with a certified team in New York City, from the first call to the report.

Before the visit

The handler will want to know the scope. How many units, which floors, whether the inspection is routine or following a complaint. They'll ask about the building, too. A prewar walk-up in Washington Heights and a new glass tower in Long Island City are different jobs.

You'll need to give tenants notice and arrange access. Unlike chemical treatment, a dog inspection usually doesn't need much prep. Ask tenants not to clean heavily or spray anything right before the visit, to keep pets secured, and to leave the apartment reasonably accessible. Strong cleaning products and fresh pesticide can make a dog's work harder.

During the search

The handler brings the dog in on a leash and works the room in a pattern, usually along baseboards, around beds and couches, near outlets, closets and furniture. The dog sniffs. When it finds bed bug odor, it gives its trained alert, sitting, pawing or freezing at the source.

A unit typically takes a few minutes. Teams rest dogs between stretches of work, especially in hot buildings. If you're doing a full building, expect the handler to pace it.

When the dog alerts

A professional handler will note the exact location and treat the alert as a strong indication, not a verdict. Many will do a quick visual check right then, or recommend that your pest control operator confirm before treatment. Good handlers will also tell you when an alert seems weak or uncertain.

The report

You should get a written report listing each unit inspected, where alerts happened, and what was confirmed. Make sure it includes the handler's name, the dog's name and the team's Tri-State K9 number. Keep it with your bed bug records.

Before you book

Look the team up in the Tri-State K9 registry and confirm that both the handler and the dog are listed and current. The point of hiring a certified team is that the dog has passed a blind field test with live bed bugs and distractors in the past year. A thirty-second lookup makes sure that's the team you're actually getting.
