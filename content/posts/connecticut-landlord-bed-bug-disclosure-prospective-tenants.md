Connecticut doesn't just regulate how landlords respond to bed bugs. It also governs what they tell people before a lease is signed. For owners who keep good records this is easy. For everyone else, it can be awkward.

## What has to be disclosed

Under §47a-7a, a landlord has to tell a prospective tenant if the unit being rented, or a unit next to it, currently has a bed bug infestation. On top of that, if the prospective tenant asks, the landlord has to give the last date the unit was inspected and found free of bed bugs.

The law also bars landlords from renting out a unit they know or suspect is infested. Disclosure isn't a way to rent an infested apartment with a warning label.

## The question you should expect

More renters know to ask about bed bugs than you might think. Online reviews, tenant forums and news coverage have made "when was this unit last inspected?" a common question at showings in New Haven, Bridgeport and Hartford.

If your answer is a shrug, the applicant will likely draw their own conclusions. If your answer is a date, ideally with a report behind it, you've turned a risk into a selling point.

## Build inspections into turnover

The cleanest approach is to inspect every unit between tenants. When a unit goes vacant, before it's painted and shown, have it checked. A certified K9 team can screen an empty unit quickly, and an empty apartment is the best possible setting for a clear result.

Record the date, the team and the result in the unit file. Then, when a prospective tenant asks, you have an answer ready.

## Why independence matters here

A date is only as good as the inspection behind it. Connecticut's statute expects detection dog teams to hold a current, independent, third-party certification. Ask any team you hire for its Tri-State K9 team number and confirm it in the public registry. Put that number on the report.

## A note on honesty

If a neighboring unit is under treatment, say so. The law requires it, and tenants who find out later tend to remember. Being straightforward, and showing what you're doing about it, usually goes over better than people expect. This is general information, not legal advice.
