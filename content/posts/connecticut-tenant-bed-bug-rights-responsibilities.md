Finding bed bugs in your apartment is stressful. In Connecticut, at least, the rules about what happens next are spelled out in state law. Section 47a-7a gives tenants specific rights, and it gives them some jobs too.

## Tell your landlord promptly

The process starts when you notify your landlord. Do it in writing, by email or letter, so there's a record of the date. Once the landlord knows, they have five business days to inspect your unit and the units next to it.

If the landlord does the inspection personally rather than using a qualified inspector, they have to give you written notice of the results within two days.

## Treatment deadlines

If bed bugs are found, treatment has to start within five business days. Landlords can hire a pest control professional or follow the statute's steps for treating it themselves, which include vacuuming and having a qualified inspector check the unit afterward. If that doesn't work, they have to bring in a licensed professional.

## Your part: preparation and access

Treatment only works when the unit is ready. Tenants are responsible for the prep the pest professional asks for, like bagging and laundering bedding and clothing and clearing space around furniture. You also need to allow reasonable access for inspection and treatment.

If you don't cooperate, the landlord can charge you for the added costs that result. If you're physically unable to do the prep, for example because of age or disability, the landlord has to arrange help. They can charge for it, but you can repay over up to six months.

## Who pays for what

Generally the landlord pays for inspection and treatment. The law doesn't require the landlord to pay for a hotel stay or to replace damaged furniture or clothing, so check your renter's insurance.

## If the landlord doesn't follow the law

Tenants can recover the greater of $250 or actual damages, plus attorney's fees. If the landlord doesn't act within the deadlines, a tenant can also end the lease with 15 days' written notice.

## One more tip

If your landlord sends a dog team, it's fair to ask for the team's Tri-State K9 number and look it up. Connecticut's law expects detection dogs to hold a current, independent certification. This is general information, not legal advice. Connecticut's Judicial Branch and local legal aid groups can help with specific situations.
