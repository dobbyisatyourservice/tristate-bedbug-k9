Most state laws don't mention bed bug dogs at all. Connecticut's does, and the wording is worth reading closely if you hire a K9 team or run one.

## The qualified inspector definition

Under §47a-7a, a landlord who gets a bed bug complaint has to inspect within five business days, either personally or through a "qualified inspector." The statute lists three kinds of qualified inspectors: a certified pesticide applicator, a local health official, and a bed bug detection team.

A bed bug detection team, in the law's words, is a scent detection canine team that holds "a current, independent, third-party certification."

Three words in that phrase do a lot of work.

## Current

A certificate from four years ago doesn't count. The team's certification has to be in effect on the day of the inspection. Dogs change, handlers change, and skills fade without practice, so an expiration date is a feature, not a formality.

## Independent

The certification can't be something the company issues to itself. A pest control firm that trains its own dog and hands it a certificate hasn't met the standard the statute describes.

## Third-party

The certifying body has to sit outside the business doing the inspection. That's the point of the Tri-State K9 Council: evaluators test teams blind, on live bed bugs, without any stake in whether the team passes.

## Why it matters in practice

When a qualified inspector finds no infestation, the statute calls for the landlord to receive written certification of that result. If that certificate later gets challenged, the first question will be whether the inspector was actually qualified. A landlord who can point to the team's current listing in a public registry is in a far better position than one who has to say, "They told us the dog was certified."

## How to check

Ask the team for its Tri-State K9 team number before the inspection. Look it up in the public registry, which shows the handler, the dog and the certification date. Then note the team number on the inspection report and keep it with the unit file. This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm specifics with a Connecticut attorney.
