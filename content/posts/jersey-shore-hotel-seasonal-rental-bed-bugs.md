From Cape May up through Monmouth County, shore properties live on turnover. A room or rental house might see a new set of guests every week from Memorial Day to Labor Day. Every one of those arrivals brings luggage, and luggage is how bed bugs travel.

## What the state already covers

New Jersey's Hotel and Multiple Dwelling Law applies to hotels and motels as well as apartment buildings. The Department of Community Affairs, through its Bureau of Housing Inspection, handles inspections of registered properties. The maintenance code's pest section, N.J.A.C. 5:10-10.2, calls for infestation prevention and elimination by qualified people at least once a year, and more often when problems recur.

Seasonal rentals are a mixed picture. A single-family shore house rented by the week isn't regulated the same way a 40-room motel is, and local rental ordinances vary by town. Owners should check with their municipality.

## A bill worth watching

In 2024, Assembly Bill A3156 was introduced in Trenton. As written, it would require eradication within 10 days of an infestation being found, with a shorter seven-day window for seasonal rentals. It hasn't become law. But it signals where legislators' attention is, and shore operators would be wise to run on something close to that timeline anyway, since a summer week lost to an infestation is revenue you don't get back.

## Use the off-season

The smartest time to look for bed bugs at the shore is in the fall and early spring, when rooms are empty and nobody's checking in. A certified K9 team can screen an entire property in a fraction of the time a room-by-room visual inspection takes, so a problem left behind in August gets caught before it spreads into next season.

During the summer, keep it targeted. When a guest complains, have the room and the rooms on either side and above and below checked before the next booking.

## Keep the paperwork

Hotels and rental managers should log every complaint, inspection and treatment by room and date. When you hire a detection team, ask for its Tri-State K9 team number and confirm it in the public registry. A dated report from a certified team is a much better answer to a guest's review, or a lawyer's letter, than "we looked." This is general information, not legal advice.
