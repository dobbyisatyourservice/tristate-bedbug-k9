The dog alerted, your pest control operator took a look, and yes, there are bed bugs in 5F. Now the clock is running. How you handle the next couple of weeks decides whether this stays a one-apartment problem or becomes a whole-line problem.

## Tell the tenant clearly and kindly

Start with the tenant in the affected unit. Explain what was found, what happens next and when. Make it clear this isn't an accusation. People bring bed bugs home from everywhere, and a tenant who feels blamed is less likely to cooperate with prep. Give written instructions, in the tenant's language, with the treatment date.

## Check the neighbors

If you haven't already screened the surrounding units, do it now. Units sharing walls, floors and ceilings with 5F are the priority. In an older building with shared risers, consider the whole F line. A certified K9 team can work through these quickly, and you'll want to know about any spread before treatment starts, so it can be coordinated.

## Coordinate treatment

Treatment in a multifamily building works best when affected units are handled together. Treating 5F while 5E quietly has an early infestation just moves the problem around. Work with your pest control operator on a schedule, confirm tenant access, and make sure prep instructions are realistic. An elderly tenant in a fifth-floor walk-up may need help.

## Follow up, more than once

One treatment rarely ends it. Schedule follow-up inspections a few weeks after treatment, and again later. Re-screen the neighbors too. Bed bugs that escaped treatment often show up next door.

## Keep the paperwork

Save everything: the original K9 report with the team's Tri-State K9 number, the confirmation, the treatment invoices, tenant notices and follow-up results. New York City requires you to disclose bed bug history to new tenants and report it to HPD annually. Those filings are only as good as the records behind them.

## Learn from it

When it's over, take ten minutes and ask how it started and how it was found. Was it a tenant report? A routine screen? Did it spread before anyone noticed? The answers tell you whether your inspection schedule is working, and whether the team you used earned a spot on your next contract.
