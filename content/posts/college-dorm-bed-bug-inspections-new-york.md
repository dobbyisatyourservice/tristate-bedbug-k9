Late August in New York is a special kind of chaos for anyone running student housing. Thousands of students arrive in a week, carrying mattress toppers from home, thrifted furniture from Craigslist, and suitcases fresh from summer travel. Then they all move into rooms that share walls, bathrooms and lounges.

If you wanted to design ideal conditions for bed bugs to spread, you'd struggle to do better.

## The calendar is the risk

Dorm bed bug problems tend to cluster around movement. Move-in. Thanksgiving and winter break, when students go home and come back. Spring break. Summer, when buildings fill with conference guests, interns and summer programs who have nothing to do with the students who'll arrive in the fall.

Each of those transitions is a chance to bring bed bugs in, and each one is also a chance to catch them early, while rooms are empty or lightly occupied.

## Where K9 inspections help

Screening empty rooms before move-in is one of the best uses of a detection dog. There's no student furniture to work around, no one to disrupt, and anything the dog finds can be handled before a new resident is exposed.

Dogs also help after a report. When a student finds bites or bugs, the housing office needs to know about the rooms on either side, above and below, quickly. A certified team can screen a cluster of rooms in the time it would take to visually inspect one or two.

## Accuracy matters more with students

Students talk. A false alert that sends a whole floor into prep mode, bagging clothes and moving out of rooms during midterms, becomes a story fast. So does a missed infestation that spreads down a hallway. Parents call. Sometimes their lawyers do.

That's why it's worth insisting on certified teams. A dog that passed a blind field test within the past year, finding live bed bugs and ignoring distractors, gives you a much better basis for decisions that affect hundreds of students.

## Put it in the contract

If your campus contracts out pest control, write certification into the scope. Require that any dog used on university property be listed in the Tri-State K9 registry with a current credential, and that the vendor provide the team number with every report.

It's a small line in a contract, and it gives your housing office something solid to point to if a family ever asks how you handle bed bugs.
