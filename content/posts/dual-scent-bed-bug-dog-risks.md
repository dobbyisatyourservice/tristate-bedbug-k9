Some detection dogs are trained to find more than one thing. Bed bugs and termites. Bed bugs and mold. It sounds efficient, one dog, two services, and for some companies it's a selling point. For the person paying for a bed bug inspection, it's a problem.

## One alert, two possible meanings

A detection dog doesn't have a separate signal for each odor. It sits, or it scratches, or it freezes, and that's the alert. If the dog was trained on bed bugs and something else, the handler can't know from the alert alone which one it found.

In practice, handlers will tell you they can tell. Maybe the context helps. Maybe they're right most of the time. But "most of the time" is a strange standard when the outcome is treating an apartment in Forest Hills, or telling a hotel to pull a room off the market on a sold-out weekend.

## Why it matters more in the city

New York buildings are dense and old. Wood framing, damp basements, shared walls. A dog trained on termites or other odors has more chances to find something in a prewar building than it would in a newer suburban house. Every one of those finds looks the same as a bed bug alert.

And the consequences in the city move fast. A bed bug alert in a multifamily building can trigger notices to neighbors, prep instructions, treatment, follow-up inspections, entries in the building's bed bug history. Unwinding that because the dog was actually reacting to something else isn't simple.

## Why the Council certifies bed-bug-only dogs

The Tri-State Bed Bug K9 Council only certifies dogs trained on bed bugs alone. When one of our certified dogs alerts, there's one odor it's been trained to report. That's the whole reason the standard exists.

It also keeps the test meaningful. Every team in our registry was tested the same way, on live bed bugs, against distractors, under the same rules. A multi-odor dog would make those results harder to compare.

## What to ask before you hire

Ask the handler directly what the dog is trained on. Not what it's used for this week. What it's ever been trained to find. If the answer includes anything besides bed bugs, you're entitled to ask how they'll tell the difference, and to decide whether that answer is good enough for your building.
