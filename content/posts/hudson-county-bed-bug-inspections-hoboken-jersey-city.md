Hudson County packs a lot of people into a small space. Brownstones in Hoboken share party walls. Jersey City's waterfront towers stack hundreds of units around the same risers. Weehawken, Union City and Bayonne add older walk-ups and mid-rise buildings. For bed bugs, that kind of density is ideal.

## How bed bugs move in dense buildings

Bed bugs don't need much room. They follow walls, slip through electrical boxes and travel along pipes. In a building where units share plumbing and heating risers, one infested apartment can become three in a matter of months.

New Jersey's maintenance code anticipates this. N.J.A.C. 5:10-10.2 requires owners to prevent vermin from moving vertically through pipe chases, and it makes the owner responsible for eradicating insects when an infestation is in two or more units or in common areas. In practice, that means an owner can't treat one apartment and call it done.

## Inspect around the problem

When a tenant reports bed bugs, the unit itself is only the start. The apartments on each side, above and below deserve a look, along with any shared laundry room or storage area. In a high-rise, check the units stacked on the same riser line.

That's where a certified K9 team saves time. A dog can work through a floor much faster than a technician pulling apart furniture, and it can find low-level activity that hasn't produced visible signs yet. The pest professional then confirms and treats.

## Tenants have a role too

Under N.J.A.C. 5:10-5.1, occupants can be held responsible for problems caused by their own willful acts or gross negligence, and in multiple dwellings owners generally have to give about a day's notice before entering for inspection or repairs. Clear notices, and a building policy that explains how inspections work, make access easier and protect both sides.

## Check the team

Hudson County has no shortage of companies offering dog inspections. Before you book one, ask for its Tri-State K9 team number and look it up in the public registry. That tells you the handler and dog were tested together on live bed bugs within the past year. This is general information, not legal advice.
