Connecticut has done something many states haven't. It asked its own scientists to write down how bed bugs should be handled, and it put that request into law.

## Where the guidance comes from

Section 22-81b of the Connecticut General Statutes directs the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station, known as CAES, to develop bed bug best management practices together with the Department of Public Health and the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection. CAES, based in New Haven, has published guidance aimed at the places where bed bugs cause the most worry: schools, child care settings and homeless shelters, among others.

## Why these settings are different

In a school or daycare, bed bugs almost always come from somewhere else. A child's coat, a backpack, a nap mat. The goal isn't usually to treat the building. It's to find out whether there's a real problem, keep a single bug from becoming a colony, and handle it without stigmatizing a family.

Shelters face the opposite challenge. People arrive constantly with everything they own, so introductions are routine. Prevention, regular monitoring and fast response matter more than any single treatment.

## Common threads in the guidance

The state's approach leans on integrated pest management. Confirm before treating. Use monitoring and inspection to find problems early. Train staff to recognize bed bugs and respond calmly. Treat with care, using licensed professionals when pesticides are needed. Applicators in Connecticut are certified through DEEP.

## Where K9 detection fits

A scent detection dog is an inspection tool, not a treatment. That makes it a natural match for an IPM approach. A certified team can screen a shelter's dormitory, a daycare's nap room or a school wing quickly and help pest professionals focus on the right spots.

Connecticut's landlord statute already recognizes detection dog teams that hold a current, independent, third-party certification. Facilities that aren't covered by that law can use the same standard. Ask any team you hire for its Tri-State K9 team number and check it in the public registry.

## Read the source

The CAES guidance is free and written for working staff rather than specialists. Facility directors should read it, share it with their pest control provider and build their written protocol around it. This is general information, not legal or medical advice.
