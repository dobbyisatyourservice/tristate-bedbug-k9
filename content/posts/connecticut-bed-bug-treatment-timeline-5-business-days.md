Connecticut's bed bug law is built around short deadlines. Property managers who know them by heart rarely have trouble. The ones who learn them after a complaint usually do. Here's how the sequence works under §47a-7a.

## Day zero: the complaint

Everything starts when a tenant tells the landlord they suspect bed bugs. Log the date and time, and how the report came in. If it came by phone, follow up in writing so both sides have a record.

## Within five business days: inspection

The landlord must inspect the reported unit and the units next to it within five business days. Business days, not calendar days, so a complaint on a Friday gives you through the following Friday.

You can inspect personally or use a qualified inspector: a certified pesticide applicator, a local health official, or a bed bug detection team with a current, independent, third-party certification. In a building with several adjacent units to check, a K9 team is often the fastest way to cover them all within the window.

## Within two days of a self-inspection: notice

If the landlord inspects personally, written notice of the results goes to the tenant within two days. When a qualified inspector finds no bed bugs, the landlord should get written certification of that result. Keep it.

## Within five business days of finding bed bugs: treatment

If there's an infestation, treatment has to happen within five business days. A licensed pest control operator is the usual route. Pesticide applicators in Connecticut are certified through the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection.

A landlord can also treat on their own, but the statute sets conditions: vacuum first, then have a qualified inspector check the unit within five business days. If that inspection still finds bed bugs, a licensed pest control agent has to be hired within another five business days.

## Follow-up

Treatment isn't the end. Schedule a post-treatment inspection, and recheck neighboring units. The last clean inspection date is something prospective tenants can ask about, so record it.

## Make the timeline routine

Keep a short list of a certified K9 team and a licensed pest control company you can call the same day. Confirm the K9 team's number in the Tri-State K9 registry before you need it. When the five-day clock starts, you won't be shopping around. This is general information, not legal advice.
