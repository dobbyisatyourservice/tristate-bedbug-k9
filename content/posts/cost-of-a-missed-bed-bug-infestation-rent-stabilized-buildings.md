Ask anyone who manages rent-stabilized housing in New York and they'll tell you the math got tighter in 2019. The state's rent law changes that year took away most of the ways owners used to recover costs on turnover. The 20 percent vacancy bonus is gone. Individual apartment improvement increases are capped at $15,000 of work over 15 years and expire after 30. Whatever a unit costs you to fix, you're largely eating it.

Now add bed bugs.

## How a small problem gets expensive

A light infestation in one apartment is a manageable job. Treatment, follow-up, maybe a second visit. The trouble starts when it isn't caught. Bed bugs travel through wall voids, along pipe chases, under doors. In older stabilized buildings, the kind with plaster walls and seventy years of patched-over penetrations, they move easily from unit to unit.

Miss it in 3C and by spring you're treating 2C, 4C, 3B and 3D. Each of those is another round of prep, another set of tenants bagging clothes and moving furniture, another chance for a complaint to land at HPD.

And in a stabilized building, you can't treat a vacancy as an opportunity to recoup. If a long-term tenant leaves because of a recurring problem, the next lease isn't going to cover the cost of fixing it.

## The hidden costs nobody budgets for

Treatment invoices are the visible part. The rest doesn't show up neatly. Staff hours coordinating access. Repeat visits when tenants aren't ready. Legal time if a tenant withholds rent or files an HP action over conditions. Units held off the market while you re-treat. Furniture you end up replacing in a furnished unit or a super's apartment.

Then there's the building's reputation. Bed bug history isn't a secret in New York. Owners have to disclose it to new tenants and report it to the city every year.

## Where detection fits

The cheapest infestation is the one you catch in a single apartment. A certified K9 team can screen a line of units quickly, including the neighbors of a known case, so you know where to treat and where you don't need to.

The key word is certified. A dog that misses light infestations doesn't save you anything. It just gives you a false sense of security until the problem shows up three doors down. A team that's passed a blind field test within the past year has at least shown it can find live bed bugs it wasn't expecting.

For rent-stabilized owners, that's not a luxury. It's one of the few cost controls you've still got.
