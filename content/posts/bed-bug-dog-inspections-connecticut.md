Connecticut doesn't get talked about much in bed bug conversations, which is part of the problem. Fairfield County sits right on the Metro-North line into Grand Central. Stamford and Norwalk have filled up with apartment towers and corporate hotels. New Haven has a major university and a lot of student and hospital-adjacent housing. Hartford has older multifamily stock and a steady flow of business travelers.

Bed bugs move with people, and plenty of people move between Connecticut and the city every day.

## The Metro-North corridor

Commuter rail is one of the quieter ways bed bugs get around the region. Bags on seats, coats on racks, the same cars carrying people from Greenwich to Midtown and back. Property managers in lower Fairfield County often see bed bug reports that look a lot like New York's, just in newer buildings with different layouts.

## Hotels, colleges and healthcare housing

Connecticut has a lot of the high-turnover housing that bed bugs love. Hotels near I-95 and Bradley Airport. Student housing in New Haven and around the state's other campuses. Apartments and extended-stay rooms near hospitals, where traveling nurses and patients' families rotate through. Each of these benefits from regular K9 screening, and each depends on the dog actually being accurate.

## One standard for the region

The Tri-State Bed Bug K9 Council certifies teams in Connecticut to the same standard as in New York and New Jersey. Two live bed bug hides, two distractors, a building of at least four rooms, about 15 minutes, two evaluators watching. Both live hides found and no more than one false response to pass. Only bed-bug-only dogs are eligible.

That means a property manager with buildings in Stamford and the Bronx can use the same registry, and the same expectations, for every team they hire.

## For Connecticut handlers

The Council holds scheduled evaluation days in Connecticut, including Fairfield County and the Hartford area, with no travel fee. If those dates don't work, an on-site evaluation at your location is available with a travel fee. The exam itself is free, and certified teams pay a $225 annual registration.

## Verify before you book

Whatever town you're in, the check is the same. Ask for the team number, search it in our registry, make sure the handler and dog match, and confirm the credential hasn't expired. It takes less time than finding parking in downtown Stamford.
