Nobody hires a bed bug dog thinking about lawyers. You hire one because there's a problem and you want it found. But in New York, bed bug decisions have a way of ending up in front of someone else: a Housing Court judge, an insurance adjuster, a hotel guest's attorney, an HPD inspector. When they do, the first question is usually how you knew what you knew.

This isn't legal advice. It's a look at where the risk tends to show up.

## When a false alert becomes a dispute

Say a dog alerts in an apartment. You order treatment, and the tenant has to bag everything, throw out a couch, miss work for prep. Later it turns out there were never bed bugs there. The tenant wants compensation, or withholds rent, or files a complaint.

If the dog was certified, you can show you relied on a team that passed a blind test recently. If it wasn't, you're explaining that you took a vendor's word for it.

## When a miss becomes negligence

The other direction is worse. A dog clears an apartment, you rent it, and the new tenant is bitten within a week. Or a hotel room is screened and a guest wakes up covered in welts.

In both cases, the question becomes whether you took reasonable steps. Using a detection method that's never been independently tested is not a great fact to defend.

## Your records are only as good as your inspector

New York City requires disclosure of bed bug history to new tenants and annual reporting to the city. Those records rely on your inspection results. If an unreliable dog put false entries in your history, or left real ones out, your paperwork is wrong in ways that are hard to fix after the fact.

## Contracts and insurance

Insurers and institutional clients may ask how your pest inspections are done. Either way, a vendor contract that requires certified teams shifts some responsibility to the vendor and gives you something concrete to point to.

## The simple fix

None of this requires anything complicated. Before you hire, look up the team in the Tri-State K9 registry. Make sure the handler and dog match and the credential is current. Save a copy of the listing with your inspection report, along with any confirmation that followed the alert.

That paper trail costs nothing, and if a bed bug dispute ever lands on your desk, it's often the first thing your attorney will ask for.
