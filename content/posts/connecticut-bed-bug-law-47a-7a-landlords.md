Connecticut is one of the few states with a law written specifically about bed bugs. Public Act 16-51 took effect on October 1, 2016, and it lives in the statutes as §47a-7a. If you rent out apartments anywhere from Stamford to Hartford to New London, it's worth knowing.

## Who it covers

The law applies to residential landlords and tenants, with one notable exception. Detached single-family homes are excluded. Multi-family buildings, including two- and three-family houses, are covered.

## The core obligations

Once a tenant tells the landlord they suspect bed bugs, the clock starts. The landlord has to inspect the unit, and the units next to it, within five business days. The landlord can do that personally or bring in a qualified inspector.

If an infestation is found, treatment has to begin within five business days as well, by a licensed pest control professional or by the landlord following specific steps. The landlord also can't rent out a unit they know or suspect is infested.

## Who pays

Generally, the landlord pays for inspection and treatment. Tenants are responsible for preparing their unit, such as bagging clothes and moving furniture, and they can be charged for added costs if they don't cooperate. The law says the landlord isn't required to pay for alternative housing or to replace a tenant's belongings.

For tenants who physically can't do the prep work, the landlord has to arrange help, but can charge for it and let the tenant repay over up to six months.

## Disclosure

Before signing a lease, a prospective tenant can learn whether the unit, or one next to it, is currently infested. On request, the landlord also has to say when the unit was last inspected and found free of bed bugs. Keeping good inspection dates on file makes that question easy to answer.

## What happens when it goes wrong

Tenants can sue for the greater of $250 or their actual damages, plus attorney's fees. Landlords who can't get access or cooperation have a court process too. Simply put, the law rewards whoever kept the better records.

## The practical takeaway

Write inspection dates down for every unit, every time. A report from a certified K9 team, with its Tri-State K9 team number on it, is the kind of dated, independent record the statute assumes you have. This is general information, not legal advice.
