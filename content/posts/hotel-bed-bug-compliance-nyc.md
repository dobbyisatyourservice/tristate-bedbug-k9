A Midtown hotel on a convention week might turn over a few hundred rooms a day. Guests come in from Penn Station, from JFK and LaGuardia, from everywhere. Some of them bring luggage that's spent the night somewhere it shouldn't have. Bed bugs don't care about star ratings.

For hotel operators, the question isn't whether bed bugs will show up. It's how fast you find them, and how few rooms you have to pull while you do.

## Why dogs make sense for hotels

Visual inspection of a hotel room is slow. Headboards come off the wall, mattresses get lifted, seams get checked with a flashlight. It's good work, and it's necessary when you've got a confirmed case. But it doesn't scale to a whole floor between checkout and check-in.

A good K9 team can screen rooms quickly and point housekeeping and pest control at the ones that need a closer look. Done regularly, it turns bed bugs from a crisis into a maintenance item.

## The cost of getting it wrong

Here's where hotels get burned. A dog alerts on a room. You pull it, maybe the adjoining rooms too, and call in treatment. If the alert was false, you've lost sellable inventory on what might be your busiest night of the month.

If the dog misses an active room, the next guest finds out for you, and sometimes they find out on social media first. Then there's the refund, the possible claim, and the review that sits at the top of your listing for a year.

Both failures come back to the same thing: whether the dog is actually accurate. That's what a blind certification is meant to measure.

## Building a hotel protocol

Hotels that handle this well tend to have a few things in common. They screen on a schedule, not just after complaints. They respond to guest reports the same day. They confirm alerts before treating. And they keep records showing who inspected what, and when.

Those records matter. If a guest claim ever turns into a legal one, being able to show regular inspections by a certified team is a lot stronger than a housekeeping log and good intentions.

## Picking the team

Before you sign a screening contract, ask for each handler's Tri-State K9 team number and look it up. Check that the dog working your property is the dog that was certified, and that the credential is current. If a vendor rotates several dogs through your building, every one of them should be listed.

New York hotels already compete on location, price and reviews. Bed bugs can wreck all three. A certified detection program is one of the cheaper ways to protect them.
