A credential card can look official and still tell you nothing. Logos are easy. Laminating machines are cheap. What matters is whether the details on it line up with a record you can check yourself.

Here's how to read a Tri-State K9 credential, line by line, and what to do if something doesn't fit.

## The team number

Every certified team gets a number in the format TSK9-YYYY-NNNN. The middle part is the year the team was listed. The last four digits are unique to that team. It's the fastest way to look someone up, so ask for it first. A real handler will rattle it off or hand you a card. Someone who gets vague here is telling you something.

## Handler and dog, together

The credential names one handler and one dog. Both names matter. Our certification covers the team as a pair, so if the person at your door isn't the listed handler, or the dog isn't the listed dog, the credential doesn't apply to today's inspection. It happens more than you'd think, especially with companies running several dogs. A company having one certified team doesn't make every team it sends certified.

## The field evaluation date

This is the day the team passed a blind field test. It's the date that actually means something, because it's when the team proved it could find live bed bugs and ignore distractors under supervision.

## The valid-through date

Credentials run 12 months from the field evaluation. After that, the team has to test again. An expired credential isn't a minor paperwork lapse. A year is a long time in a working dog's life, and it's plenty of time for a handler's habits to drift.

## Check it against the registry

Never rely on the card alone. Go to the Certified Teams page on our site and search the team number, the handler's name or the dog's name. The registry is the record. If the card says one thing and the registry says another, trust the registry.

If the team isn't listed at all, its Tri-State K9 credential isn't current. That doesn't automatically mean they're bad at their job, but it does mean you're taking their word for it.

## Keep a copy

Property managers in Manhattan and Brooklyn who do this well keep a screenshot of the registry listing with each inspection report. It takes ten seconds, and if a tenant dispute or an HPD complaint ever comes up, you'll have a dated record showing you hired a verified team.
