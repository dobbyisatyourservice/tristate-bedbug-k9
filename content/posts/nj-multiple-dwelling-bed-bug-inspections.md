If you own a building with three or more apartments in New Jersey, the state already knows about you. Or it should. Under the Hotel and Multiple Dwelling Law, those buildings are registered with the Department of Community Affairs and inspected by its Bureau of Housing Inspection on a five-year cycle.

That inspection is a good moment to think about bed bugs, because the maintenance code the Bureau enforces has specific pest rules.

## Who's covered

The law covers multiple dwellings, meaning buildings with three or more dwelling units, along with hotels and motels. Condominiums and co-ops can count too, though some small, owner-occupied buildings are exempt from parts of the law. If you're not sure where your building falls, the Bureau's own site is the place to check.

## What the code expects on pests

The pest section of the maintenance regulations, N.J.A.C. 5:10-10.2, puts responsibility for eradicating insects on the owner when an infestation is in two or more units or in common areas. It also calls for infestation prevention and elimination by qualified people at least once a year, more often if problems keep coming back, and for blocking vermin from moving between floors through pipe chases.

So the question an inspector, or a tenant's attorney, may eventually ask isn't "did you have bed bugs?" It's "what were you doing about pests, and can you show it?"

## Five years is a long time

A five-year cycle means a lot can happen between visits. A building can go from clean to a full-blown problem in a single summer. Owners who wait for the state inspection to find out how they're doing are taking a real risk.

## Building a record that holds up

The owners who do best keep a simple file for every building. It includes the annual pest prevention service, every tenant complaint, inspection reports with dates and units, confirmations and treatments.

Certified K9 screening fits neatly into that file. A detection team can check common areas and units efficiently, and a report from a team listed in the Tri-State K9 registry shows that whoever looked for bed bugs had been independently tested on live bed bugs within the past year. Write the team number on the report. When the next inspection comes around, you'll have more than a receipt from an exterminator. You'll have a record of how you actually looked.
