An empty apartment is the easiest place in the world to find bed bugs. No furniture to move, no tenant to schedule around, no belongings piled against the walls. It's also the last good chance to find them before someone new moves in and the problem becomes theirs, and then yours again.

## The turnover gap

When a tenant moves out of a New York apartment, the unit usually gets a quick paint job, maybe new appliances, a cleaning, and a broker's photos. Bed bugs can survive all of that. They hide in baseboards, outlet boxes, door frames and the gaps around radiator pipes. They can go a long time without feeding, waiting for the next person to lie down.

A new tenant who finds bed bugs in the first month won't believe they brought them. They might be right. Either way, you're now treating an occupied unit, possibly the neighbors' units, and starting a tenancy on the worst possible note.

## Why dogs work well here

Screening a vacant unit takes a certified K9 team a few minutes. With the place empty, the dog can cover every wall, closet and corner without obstacles. If it alerts, you can confirm and treat before anyone moves in. If it doesn't, you've got a documented clean inspection on the day you handed over the keys.

## It protects your disclosure records

New York City requires owners to tell prospective tenants about the building's bed bug history and to report infestation history to the city each year. A vacancy screen gives you current information for both. It also gives you a solid answer if a new tenant later claims the apartment was infested when they moved in.

## Make it routine, not reactive

The owners who get the most out of vacancy screening do it every time a unit turns over, not just when a previous tenant complained. Bed bugs aren't always reported. Some tenants never noticed. Some noticed and didn't want the hassle.

Add it to your turnover checklist, right after the paint and before the photos. Book a certified team, verify them in the Tri-State K9 registry, and keep the report with the lease file. It's one of the cheapest pieces of insurance you can buy in this city.
